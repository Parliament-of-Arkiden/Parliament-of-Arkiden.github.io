  <summary>Adriatic State Autonomy Reevaluation</summary>

Executive Order to re-evaluate state autonomy and right to secede.

As President, I would like to declare a special autonomy status for the state of the Adriatic Island, given its deeply unique interests and large distance from the mainland.
If the Adriatic Island wishes to secede from Arkiden, the state owner may choose to do so if a state referendum achieves a simple majority, followed by Presidential approval.
No Parliamentary voting will be used for the independence of the Adriatic Island.
If other states would like to secede, a parliamentary vote must be held with a simple majority to accept secession.
Secession does not mean a state should be removed from the community. Independence cannot be revoked by a future government.

Signed,
President Daniel

  <summary>Adriatic Firearms Act</summary>

Within the state of the Adriatic Island, it is legal to own only a handgun or rifle with clear hunting purposes. Weapons heavier than this may not be owned by ordinary civilians and can carry up to 10 years in prison.

It is also legal to own and carry any knife or legal firearms in public, given it is not directly used to intimidate, harm, or threaten.

**Sales and licensing**

- Legal firearms may only be bought from registered and licensed shops, which must keep records of their sales and be inspected unexpectedly to check for health and safety standards and compliance with the law.
- To purchase a firearm, you must have a licence, which can be obtained by request to the government.
- A licence may only be blocked for criminal or mental health reasons, or for connections to people with those problems.
- A training course must be completed before the licence is granted.

<details>
  <summary>Adriatic Trade Protection Act</summary>

To encourage the self-sufficiency of the Adriatic economy in core domestic sectors such as farming, construction, and fishing, the government will take the following measures to support Adriatic-based businesses:

1. 25% tariffs on all fish not caught by Adriatic fishermen
2. Abolishing inheritance tax on vessels worth under $100,000
3. Abolishing VAT on fishing and farming equipment
4. Imposing a 25% tariff on foreign tropical food products, such as sugar, bananas, mangoes, etc.

<details>
  <summary>Fort Archie Business Act</summary>

An Act to regulate commercial enterprises within the Fort of Duke Archie, establish fair corporate taxation, and secure funding for public infrastructure and island defense.

This law applies to all commercial entities, trade ventures, and service providers operating within the territorial boundaries of Fort Archie.

**Tax tiers**

Commercial entities operating within the territory are categorised into four distinct tax tiers based on their average monthly gross revenue:

- **Tier 1: Small Business** (under $5,000 / month)
  - Tax rate: 0% (tax-exempt)
- **Tier 2: Medium Business** ($5,000 to $10,000 / month)
  - Tax rate: 2% flat corporate tax on gross revenues
- **Tier 3: Large Business** ($10,001 to $40,000 / month)
  - Tax rate: 5% flat corporate tax on gross revenues
- **Tier 4: Extra Large / Enterprise** ($40,000+ / month)
  - Tax rate: 7% flat corporate tax on gross revenues

**Purpose:** The tax is to support growing local enterprises while contributing lightly to island upkeep.

**Exceptions**

- Anything outside of Fort Archie territory
- Local agricultural innovation
- Renewable energy production
- Direct logistics, equipment maintenance, or supply manufacturing for the State Guard
- Military businesses

</details>

<details>
  <summary>Adriatic Immigration Points System Act</summary>

To cope with the high amount of immigration, of which a ridiculously large proportion are foreign pensioners who do not pay taxes, and to preserve the native people of the Adriatic Island, the government will use a new points-based system for immigration.

- There will be a hard cap of 100,000 immigrants of any kind per year, prioritised through points awarded based on qualifications and connections to the state.
- The City of Port Jack University will accept as many native Adriatic Islanders as it fairly can before allowing foreign students to attend.
- Current immigrants who have settled legally will not be deported or required to leave.
- To temporarily support the high proportion of pensioners, the Adriatic Island will offer work visas with the opportunity to settle to foreign people looking for opportunities to become apprentices in highly specialised careers, for the next 5 years only.

</details>

<details>
  <summary>Rainforest Preservation Act</summary>

To preserve the unique biodiversity and beauty of the Adriatic Island's rainforests, this law will enable the government to declare green belts, which are large areas of land where development and resource extraction of any sort is prohibited.

Green belts will be in 3 tiers:

- **Tight Belt:** No development of any kind allowed
- **Normal Belt:** Development requires special permission from the government
- **Loose Belt:** Development needs specific considerations to local wildlife

<details>
  <summary>FMTC Healthcare System</summary>

**FMTC regulation**

The government sets a maximum patient cost for each treatment. Instead of capping the provider's profit directly, the government subsidises part of the cost based on patient income.

*Example:* the treatment cost is $10,000. The government reimburses $10,000 plus a bonus if recovery rates are high and complications are low. If a provider charges above $10,000 without a valid reason, the extra amount is rejected.

**Private healthcare regulations**

*(These can differ depending on the country's economy.)*

**1st Grade:** Located in big cities.
- At least 8 ORs, 2 traumatology rooms, and 2 cardiology rooms (14 surgery rooms in total)
- At least 4 CT machines and 6 X-rays
- Medicines must meet the same standards as government-owned hospitals
- Same ICU standards (at least 2 rooms) and at least 2 recovery rooms
- Rooms for each of the following: CCU, isolation, diagnostic imaging, conferences, family, anaesthesia carts, and medical records (which hold records and can print them out)
- Must have 3-5 ECMOs or more

**2nd Grade:** Mostly for smaller towns and cities.
- At least 4 surgery rooms, including 2 ORs and 2 for cardiology and traumatology
- Medicines must meet public hospital standards
- At least 1-2 X-rays and CT machines
- An ICU, CCU, isolation rooms, and basic rooms for diagnosing and treating light conditions

**3rd Grade:** For small villages.
- Medicines must meet public hospital standards
- Can perform light treatments such as bandaging and treating fevers or colds
- Cannot perform surgeries, other than handling dislocated joints or setting broken bones

**Enforcement**

- If standards are not met, the hospital loses its licence.
- If negligence causes a death, those responsible face 5 years to life in prison and a minimum fine of $200,000 USD.
- Pharmacies may sell medicines as long as they come from legally licensed sources.
- All medicines and medical supplies must come from legally registered and licensed companies or the government. Violating this results in a minimum fine of $100,000 USD.
- Tax for private healthcare is 5-10%.

**Eligibility**

The FMTC ensures patients have enough funds to pay. Patients may choose to pay themselves.

Some people may not receive FMTC treatment:

- People from other countries without citizenship
- People who entered the country illegally
- People who own more than 3,000,000 IGD in total assets

**Co-pay**

People need to co-pay around $5-10 for GP visits. This excludes:

- Disabled people
- Children (under 18)
- Low-income families (earning 10% above minimum wage or less)

**National Health Oversight Board**

- Inspects hospitals
- Controls licensing
- Reviews complaints
- Punishes negligence

The board is completely separate from the government providers. It exists to inspect them, report problems, and deal with them.

**Monitors for FMTC**

- Billing vs. government rates
- Treatment quality
- Patient outcomes

All hospitals must report detailed cost breakdowns, which are publicly viewable.

<details>
  <summary>Marriage</summary>

Ceremony and legal marriage are separate. To marry legally, both people must be:

- Over 18
- Not biologically related (e.g., cousins, aunts, uncles, siblings, parents)
- Living together for at least 3 months

They must provide documents and proof to the government to receive a marriage certificate. Any type of ceremony is allowed.

**Divorce**

- The side that cheated forfeits all money and property gained during the marriage and receives no support.
- The more mentally and economically stable parent gains custody.
- Money earned before the marriage remains separate.
- Money earned during the marriage is divided by contribution (e.g., 60/40).

<details>
  <summary>Land Ownership Act</summary>

People have the right to own land but must prove that the land will be used (e.g., with a blueprint or plan).

**Unused land**

If land is left unused and empty for 40 years, the government can reclaim and resell it. The original owner receives compensation of roughly 75-100% of the land's total worth.

**Buildings and compensation**

- Buildings will not be forcefully nationalised.
- If the government needs the land or building, it must pay 100-125% of the property's worth.
- If the government needs to demolish a building, it must pay 90-110% of the total worth, along with rehousing for displaced residents.

**Tax**

- All tax collected goes straight back to the local state council.
- No normal tax is paid just for owning land.
- There is no typical yearly property tax (e.g., 1.5% per year). Properties are not taxed for existing; the transitions are taxed instead: 5% for urban areas and 3% for rural areas.

</details>
<details>
  <summary>Workers' Protection Laws</summary>

- The minimum wage is set at 13 RAD per hour.
- Those under 14 may not work.
- Workers under 16 are paid a minimum wage of 10 RAD.
- Overtime is paid at 125%+ of normal wages.

**Injury at work**

If a worker is injured at work and their future employment is negatively affected (e.g., losing a finger or limb), they must receive at least 200,000 RAD in compensation from the company.

**Death at work**

If a worker dies during work, the company will be investigated, and compensation must be paid to the family of not less than 500,000 RAD (depending on company size).

<details>
  <summary>Right to Bear Arms</summary>

This law gives all citizens the right to own weapons, but they must be light arms (mainly firearms). The law exists to protect the safety of the people of Arkiden.

**Right to use your weapon**

Citizens may defend themselves with their weapons if their property is being trespassed on without the owner's permission. They may also defend themselves if they receive an immediate and unlawful physical attack.

*Meaning of threat:* a living creature that is physically attacking the citizen or the citizen's property.

Before shooting, the citizen must warn the threat and at least shout "I'll shoot if you don't stop". If the danger continues, they have the right to shoot.

**Restrictions on ownership**

Those deemed mentally unstable, disabled, or ill by a hospital or proper medical care do not have the right to own a weapon. Any weapon must not be stored in the same building as people who are mentally unstable, ill, or disabled. In short:

1. Ownership restrictions only by court order
2. Based on documented medical assessment
3. Right to appeal

Those imprisoned for violence-related crimes for more than 5 years are not allowed to own any weapons.

**Right to carry a weapon in public**

You may transport a weapon in public with a valid reason (e.g., taking a gun to the local hunting club, or taking a newly bought gun home).

When transporting in public, the ammunition and the gun itself must be kept separate. Carrying a firearm for self-defence in public is illegal.

Defending your own home with a firearm is legal.
